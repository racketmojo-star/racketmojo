Here's a secret most racket manufacturers don't want you to know: **the difference between a $90 racket and a $250 racket is mostly marketing, not performance** — especially at the beginner and intermediate level.

Yes, premium materials like 12K carbon and MultiEVA cores add performance. But for the vast majority of players, a well-made fiberglass racket with a comfortable core will serve you brilliantly. And your wallet will thank you.

Here are the best padel rackets that deliver genuine quality without the premium price tag.

## When to Spend More (and When Not To)

Spend more when: you're playing 3+ times a week, your technique is consistent, and you can feel the difference between racket characteristics. At that point, premium materials genuinely improve your game.

Don't spend more when: you're new to padel, play casually, or can't yet articulate what you want different from your current racket. A $90 racket that matches your style will outperform a $300 racket that doesn't.

And matching racket to style — that's the hard part. Spec sheets tell you weight and shape, but they can't tell you how a racket will feel in YOUR hands based on YOUR swing. That kind of personalization is exactly why recommendation tools exist.
