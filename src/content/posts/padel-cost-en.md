Padel is often marketed as the "cheaper cousin" of tennis. But is it actually cheap? The honest answer: it depends on where you live, how often you play, and what you buy. Here's the real picture for 2026.

## The Real Numbers: What Things Cost


Once you know the budget, the next step is finding courts near you. [<strong>Read more about the court setup here</strong>](/en/blog/where-to-play/)

| Item | Typical price range | Notes |
|------|--------------------:|-------|
| Court rental (per hour, per person) | 5–15 EUR | Split between 4 players |
| Racket | 60–400 EUR | Beginners: 60–150 EUR |
| Balls (tube of 3) | 15–24 EUR (5–8 EUR each) | One tube lasts ~4–6 sessions |
| Shoes | 80–200 EUR | Court-specific soles |
| Club membership (monthly) | 30–100 EUR | Optional, often discounted rates |
| Coaching (per hour) | 30–60 EUR | Group lessons cost less per person |

## Country by Country: How Prices Compare

Where you play changes the numbers dramatically. Spain remains the cheapest major market thanks to sheer court supply, while premium destinations like Dubai sit at the top.

| Region | Court/hr/person | Membership/month | Notes |
|--------|----------------:|-----------------:|-------|
| Spain | 5–10 EUR | 30–60 EUR | Cheapest, most courts |
| LATAM (Argentina, Mexico) | 4–8 EUR | 25–50 EUR | Very affordable |
| UAE (Dubai) | 15–25 EUR | 80–150 EUR | Premium, mostly indoor |
| North America (US, Canada) | 12–20 EUR | 50–100 EUR | Fast growth, often indoor |
| Asia (Japan, Korea) | 10–18 EUR | 60–120 EUR | Indoor-heavy markets |

## One-Time vs Recurring Costs

To see the true picture, separate startup costs from what you'll pay every month:

| Cost type | Amount | Details |
|-----------|-------:|---------|
| Startup (racket, shoes, bag) | 150–500 EUR | One-time purchase |
| Monthly (courts, balls) | 40–120 EUR | 2–3 sessions per week |
| Yearly total (casual player) | 600–1,500 EUR | Includes gear replacement |

A casual player in Spain might spend under 700 EUR in year one; the same player in Dubai or New York can easily pass 2,000 EUR. Neither is "expensive" compared to golf or skiing — but the range is wide.

## Where You Can Save

- **Play off-peak:** morning and lunchtime rates are often 30–50% cheaper than evening slots.
- **Share balls:** one tube of three lasts several sessions across four players.
- **Skip the pro racket:** a 100–150 EUR racket suits 90% of players better than a 300 EUR model.
- **Take group lessons:** sharing a coach between 2–4 players cuts the cost per person dramatically.

The smartest way to keep padel affordable is to spend only where it improves your game — and that starts with matching your equipment to your actual level and style. Most players overspend on rackets that are too advanced for them, then wonder why their game hasn't improved. Spending less isn't about cutting corners; it's about buying the right thing for you. That's where a personalized approach beats generic advice: knowing exactly what fits your game — and your budget — saves you money from the very first purchase.
