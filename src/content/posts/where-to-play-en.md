Padel has gone from a niche Spanish pastime to one of the world's fastest-growing sports. By 2026, more than 25 million people play regularly, and new courts are opening every month — from converted tennis clubs in Scandinavia to purpose-built centers in Dubai and Mexico City.

But here's the paradox: the sport has never been more available, yet finding the right court for you can still feel like a scavenger hunt. Whether you're a complete beginner or relocating to a new city, this guide shows you exactly how to find courts near you — and how to pick the ones worth your money.

## Start With the Official Sources

The most reliable way to find real, playable courts is to skip the generic search results and go straight to the federations. The International Padel Federation (FIP) maintains a directory of affiliated federations and clubs across more than 70 countries. From there, national bodies — like the RFEP in Spain or the FIP-affiliated federations across Latin America — publish searchable club lists with addresses, contact details, and competition calendars.

| Source | What it finds | Best for |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| FIP International Padel Federation | Federations and affiliated clubs worldwide | Travelers and newcomers |
| National federation directories | Licensed clubs, tournaments, coaches | Players who want official venues |
| Local tourism boards | Pop-up and resort courts | Holiday players |

Federation listings aren't always perfectly up to date, but they guarantee the court actually exists — a real advantage over random search results.

## Google Maps: The Practical Search

For day-to-day discovery, Google Maps is still the fastest tool. Search "padel club near me" and you'll get a list ranked by distance, with photos, ratings, and — increasingly — direct booking links.

### Search keywords that work

- "padel court rental" + your city
- "club de pádel" in Spanish-speaking countries
- "padel indoor" to filter for covered courts
- "padel beginner classes" if you want lessons too

Check recent reviews for court condition, lighting, and how busy the place gets. A 4.8-star club ten minutes away often beats a 4.2-star one across town.

## Indoor vs Outdoor Courts

This choice affects price, comfort, and how often you can play.

| Factor | Indoor | Outdoor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Price per hour | Higher (10–20 EUR) | Lower (5–12 EUR) |
| Weather | Playable year-round | Depends on climate |
| Surface play | Slower, more controlled | Faster, wind affects play |
| Booking pressure | High in the evenings | Moderate |

In hot countries like Spain or the UAE, serious players favor indoor or shaded courts during summer months. In cooler climates, outdoor courts work fine most of the year — and cost noticeably less.

## Local Sports Centers and Booking Apps

Municipal sports centers are the hidden gems of padel. Many cities subsidize court rates, making them 30–50% cheaper than private clubs. Tennis clubs increasingly convert one or two courts to padel to meet demand — often with better maintenance than dedicated commercial centers.

For booking, specialized apps have transformed the experience. Playtomic dominates Spain and Latin America, letting you book, pay, and find partners in one place. Matchi covers much of Northern Europe, while platforms like CourtReserve are common in North America.

| App | Region | Strength |
|-----|--------|----------|
| Playtomic | Spain, LATAM | Partner matching + booking |
| Matchi | UK, Northern Europe | Large court network |
| CourtReserve | North America | Club management + booking |

### What to check before you book

1. Confirm the surface — artificial grass plays differently from synthetic turf.
2. Ask about guest policies if you're not a member.
3. Book off-peak (mornings, mid-afternoon) to save 30–50%.

The right court for you depends on your level, your budget, and your schedule — and so does the right gear. A court that suits a weekend social player feels very different from one a competitive player would choose, and the same logic applies to the equipment you bring onto it. Matching your setup to your actual game — your level, how often you play, your style — matters more than copying what anyone else uses. That's the kind of personal matchmaking a quick assessment can do for you, pointing you to the court and the kit that genuinely fit.
