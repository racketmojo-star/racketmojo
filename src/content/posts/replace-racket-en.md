Padel rackets don't last forever. Unlike tennis rackets that can survive years, padel rackets take a beating — every smash, every wall impact, every ball compresses the core a little more. Eventually, the performance drops, even if there's no visible damage.

Here are the five signs it's time for a new racket.

---

## 1. Visible Cracks or Chips

This one's obvious. If you see cracks in the frame, chips in the surface, or the face starting to separate from the frame — it's done. Playing with a cracked racket isn't just bad for performance, it's dangerous. A racket that breaks mid-swing can injure you or someone on court.

**Action:** Replace immediately. Don't try to glue it.

---

## 2. Dead Feel on Impact

This is the most common sign experienced players notice. Shots that used to feel crisp now feel dull. The ball comes off the racket with less pace, and you feel like you're working harder to generate the same power.

The foam core inside your racket compresses over time. Once it loses its ability to spring back, the racket feels "dead." There's no fixing this — it's permanent material fatigue.

**Action:** If you're consistently feeling less pop on your shots over several sessions, the core has degraded. Time to replace.

---

## 3. Increased Vibration

A healthy racket absorbs most of the vibration from ball impact. As the core breaks down, more vibration travels up the handle into your arm. If your elbow or wrist hurts more than usual after playing — and you haven't changed your technique or playing frequency — your racket may be the culprit.

**Action:** Test with a friend's racket. If the pain disappears, your racket's core is shot.

---

## 4. Rough / Worn Surface

Padel rackets with textured surfaces (carbon, rough finishes) help generate spin. Over time, the texture wears smooth — especially in the sweet spot area where you make most contact. Once the roughness is gone, you lose spin potential.

Run your fingernail across the surface. If the sweet spot feels noticeably smoother than the edges, the racket's spin capability has degraded.

**Action:** For casual players, this isn't critical. For competitive players who rely on spin, it's a performance loss worth addressing.

---

## 5. It's Been 12–18 Months of Regular Play

Even without visible damage, a racket played 2–3 times a week will degrade within 12–18 months. The foam compresses, the surface wears, and the frame accumulates micro-damage you can't see. If you're approaching or past this window and feeling like your game has plateaued, a fresh racket might unlock improvement.

**Action:** If you're in this window, try a demo racket. You might be surprised at how much livelier a new racket feels.

---

## The Replace-or-Repair Decision

Spoiler: you almost never repair a padel racket. Unlike tennis rackets that can be re-strung, padel rackets are sealed units. Once the core goes or the frame cracks, the racket is finished.

The good news? A new racket doesn't have to be expensive. And if you're due for a replacement, it's the perfect moment to reassess whether your current racket actually matches your playing style — because your game has probably evolved since you bought it.
